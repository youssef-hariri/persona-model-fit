# Change Log — "Doing More with Less: How Cultural Personas Bridge the Cost-Performance Gap in LLMs"

**Draft:** `paper4_v2_corrected.tex` (revised September 25, 2026), produced from `paper4_to_fix.tex` (Overleaf export, verified sentence-for-sentence identical to the submitted PDF `Youssef_Hariri_doing_more_with_less_llm_personas.pdf`).

**Method of correction:** 111 exact-match replacements applied by `apply_fixes.py`, a fail-loud script that aborts unless every anchor matches the source exactly once. Every reported number was recomputed from the released evaluation data and traces to a frozen artifact in `recompute/`, generated by `recompute_all.py` (pipeline log: `recompute/run_log.txt`) and `recompute_robustness.py`. Both pipelines re-run bit-identical. Figures were regenerated at the corrected analysis grain into `figures_v2/` by `generate_figures_v2.py`; original figures are untouched.

**Central correction — unit of analysis.** The submitted draft computed all inferential statistics at the *rating* grain (~900 observations per cell), treating the nine judge ratings of each response as independent observations. The correct unit is the *response* (the mean of its nine judge ratings; N = 3,516 responses, ~100 per cell, 2,818 in the persona × region analyses). All statistics below are response-level unless noted.

### 1.2 Inter-rater reliability (Table 1, abstract, §3.6, §4 opening, §5.1.4, limitations, A.7, A.8)
- The claim "Novelty was the only dimension with good agreement (κ = 0.68)" is removed. The 0.68 traces to a 35-response subset (`results_v2/inter_rater_reliability.csv`: 0.679); across 200 random 35-response subsets κ(Novelty) ranges 0.16–0.71 (median 0.39) — the published value was an upper-tail draw of an unstable subset estimate.
- Full-data Fleiss' κ (all 3,516 responses): 9-rater grain **0.33 / 0.32 / 0.32 / 0.31 / 0.17**; 3-judge-means grain 0.40 / 0.26 / 0.31 / 0.30 / 0.07 (Novelty / Usefulness / Flexibility / Elaboration / Cultural Sensitivity). The four core dimensions are fair-to-moderate and statistically indistinguishable; the Novelty focus is re-justified on theoretical grounds (it is the construct central to the research question), with other dimensions reported as exploratory.
- The subset history and instability are disclosed in §3.6.

**Evidence:** `recompute/fleiss_kappa.csv` (R7); `results_v2/inter_rater_reliability.csv` via `corrected_inter_rater.py`; subset analysis documented in `CORRECTIONS_MEMO.md`.

### 1.3 Regional moderation (Table 4, Table 5, §4.2.1–4.2.2, §5.1.2, abstract)
- Submitted Table 4 reported a marginal persona × region interaction (p = 0.052) that matched **neither** analysis grain of the released data; it came from a superseded data extract. Replaced with the response-level two-way ANOVA: persona F(3, 2810) = 35.75, region F(1, 2810) = 61.51, interaction **F(3, 2810) = 0.89, p = 0.445**. The old value and its provenance are disclosed in the table note.
- The apparent rating-level interaction (binary spec, t = −3.41) is identified in-text as a pseudo-replication artifact.
- Moderation regression (response level): interaction t = −1.27, p = 0.206; BF = 0.331. Persona-specific interactions non-significant (Culture_5 × East p = 0.935; Culture_Expert × East p = 0.383; HC3-robust p = 0.349). The earlier claim "Eastern models respond more strongly to Culture_Expert" does not survive correction at either grain and is retracted.
- Descriptive region effects corrected: Culture_5 +0.449 (Western) vs +0.218 (Eastern); Culture_Expert +0.140 vs +0.050; Culture_11 −0.213 vs −0.316.
- ANOVA assumption violations now disclosed (Shapiro–Wilk p < 0.001; Levene p = 0.003), with inference resting on bootstrap CIs.

**Evidence:** `recompute/anova_response.csv`, `recompute/moderation_response.csv` (R5, R6); assumption tests in `recompute_robustness.py` output (B5).
